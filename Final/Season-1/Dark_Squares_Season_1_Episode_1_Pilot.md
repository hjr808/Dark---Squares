## COLD OPEN / TEASER

**FADE IN:**

### EXT. THE WOODS — NEIGHBORHOOD STREET — EARLY EVENING

The neighborhood settles into the hour before streetlights. Not quiet. Not loud.
The specific frequency of a block that is always paying attention.

A four-family building. Low and wide. The kind of building that has been here
longer than any of its current residents.

A DOG — small, tan, watchful — sits on the front step. This is **KIZZY**. She is
facing the street.

A BOY, fourteen, comes around the corner. This is **MANNY**. He is not rushing.
He is reading. His eyes move over the block the way someone reads a room they have
been in a hundred times: not looking for anything new, but confirming that nothing
old has changed.

He checks the building's front door.
He checks the driveway.
He checks the corner where the older boys sometimes stand.

Corner is clear.

He relaxes — slightly, not completely. Relaxing completely is not something you do
in the Woods.

Kizzy gets up and moves to him.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
The Woods taught me something school never did. It taught me how to read a face
before the face decided what it was going to be. Taught me how to cross the street
before a situation became a situation. Taught me that a small thing — a wrong look,
a borrowed dollar, a girl someone decided was spoken for — could follow you to your
front door if you weren't paying attention.

*(beat)*

Most people call it a neighborhood. We called it the test. The test never stopped.

Manny reaches the front step. He sits next to Kizzy. She leans against him. He
watches the street.

From inside the building — from the apartment low in the building, near the basement
hallway — the sound of a television.

**CUT TO TITLE: DARK SQUARES**

---

## ACT ONE

### INT. ELLA MAE'S APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS

Small. Specific. Everything in it earned.

**ELLA MAE**, late thirties, moves through the kitchen with the efficiency of a
person who has a shift to get to and a house to hold together before she leaves.

**KEITH**, ten years old, sits at the kitchen table with his homework in front of
him and his eyes on something else entirely.

**ELLA MAE**
Keith. That homework.

**KEITH**
I'm doing it.

**ELLA MAE**
You're looking at the wall.

**KEITH**
I do both.

Ella Mae gives him a look. The look does not require words. Keith looks at the
homework.

**ELLA MAE**
Manny's home?

**KEITH**
He's on the steps.

She nods. She moves to the window. She can see the front step from here — just
barely, at an angle. Manny and Kizzy, watching the street.

Something in her face: relief she will not say out loud.

---

### INT. ELLA MAE'S APARTMENT — DOORWAY — MOMENTS LATER

Ella Mae stands in the doorway in her work uniform. Work bag at her shoulder. She
looks at Manny and Keith until they are both listening.

**ELLA MAE**
The house rule.

This is not a question.

**MANNY**
The house rule. Yes.

**ELLA MAE**
What is it?

**MANNY**
Whatever's out there stays out there.

**ELLA MAE**
*Whatever's out there stays out there.* And?

**MANNY**
And if you bring it in, it's on you.

She holds his look for a moment.

**ELLA MAE**
Don't be out late. Streetlights come on, you're in.

**MANNY**
I know.

**ELLA MAE**
I know you know. Say it anyway.

**MANNY**
Streetlights come on, we're in.

She pulls the door behind her. The lock clicks.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
She did not say it like a suggestion. She said it like scripture.

Manny sits with Kizzy. The street settles into the hour.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Whatever happened outside stayed outside. Whatever pride, disrespect,
misunderstanding, or trouble followed a person home had to die before it reached
her living room.

Keith appears at the doorway, watching his brother.

---

### EXT. THE WOODS — LATER

Manny and Keith ride bikes through the block. Two boys in motion, reading the
neighborhood the same way their mother reads them: watching for what changed,
confirming what is still the same.

Kizzy races behind them.

They pass a stickball game. A basketball bouncing on cracked pavement. Older boys
carrying a boom box.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Manny understood that rule better than anyone. He was the oldest. Keith was his
little brother. Four years separated them, but in the Woods, four years could feel
like a whole lifetime.

Keith swerves close to Manny, grinning. Manny reaches out and steadies him before
he can clip the curb.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Manny was not Keith's father. But he carried pieces of that job anyway.

Manny watches Keith stand on the pedals, arms out like wings.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Keith followed crowds easily. People liked him. He could make anybody laugh, and
that gift made him welcome in places Manny did not always want him to be.

---

### EXT. EMPTY LOT / ALLEY — LATE AFTERNOON

Keith hears the boom box from the corner and starts moving to the beat. He tries
to make Manny laugh with a ridiculous dance. Manny does, despite himself.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Music was already there, even then. Coming from car windows. From radios on
porches. From older boys carrying boom boxes. Hip-hop was not background noise
to them. It was weather. It was the sound of growing up.

Manny and Keith pause at the mouth of the alley, listening.

Older kids call Keith over. Keith heads toward them without thinking. Manny catches
the back of his shirt.

**MANNY**
Not over there.

**KEITH**
I'm just talking.

**MANNY**
Talk from here.

Keith rolls his eyes, but stays. Kizzy plants herself beside Manny's leg.

The older boys lose interest and move on.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Some boys attract friends. Some attract trouble. Keith had a way of attracting both.

---

## ACT TWO

### EXT. THE WOODS — NIGHT

The streetlights are on. The boys should be home. Instead they have found a
comfortable pocket at the edge of the block where the rules feel slightly further
away.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
When Ella Mae worked late, the boys had more freedom than they should have had.
The neighborhood opened up after the streetlights came on.

A boom box somewhere down the block. The older boys again, moving slow.

Keith watches them. Something in his body wants to follow.

**KEITH**
You know Steve?

**MANNY**
Which Steve.

**KEITH**
The one with all the records. He's got turntables in his room. James said he lets
people come listen.

Manny files this away. Does not respond immediately.

**KEITH**
You ever see him work?

**MANNY**
No.

**KEITH**
James said it sounds like a whole radio station.

Manny looks toward the older boys. Then back at his brother.

**MANNY**
You doing your homework?

**KEITH**
Why you always change the subject?

**MANNY**
Because when I ask about homework and you don't answer, that tells me something.

Keith makes a face. Kicks a stone down the alley. Kizzy chases it.

**KEITH**
Homework's done.

**MANNY**
Then we can go in.

---

### EXT. THE WOODS — CONTINUOUS

The brothers ride side by side beneath the streetlights. Their building waits ahead.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
In those days, the Woods felt bigger than the whole world. They had no idea they
would one day move away from it.

Keith laughs at something only he understands. Manny watches him, protective and
quiet.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
They had no idea the Woods would follow them anyway.

Kizzy runs between the bikes as the boys reach the front of the building.

They stop. Manny looks at the corner one more time. Still clear.

**KEITH**
You ever scared?

The question comes from nowhere. Manny looks at him.

**MANNY**
Of what.

**KEITH**
I don't know. Everything.

Manny considers this. Does not rush the answer.

**MANNY**
Yeah. Sometimes.

**KEITH**
What do you do?

**MANNY**
Keep moving. Read the room. Make sure you're with me.

Keith absorbs this.

**KEITH**
That's it?

**MANNY**
That's a lot.

Keith nods, satisfied. Kizzy noses at the building's front door.

---

## ACT THREE

### EXT. THE WOODS — THE FOLLOWING DAYS

A MONTAGE of the brothers' ordinary life in the Woods:

— Manny helps Keith with math at the kitchen table. Keith draws pictures in the
margins when he thinks Manny isn't looking. Manny sees. Says nothing.

— The brothers play stickball in the alley with neighborhood kids. Manny lets Keith
win a close call. Keith does not notice.

— Manny walks the long way around the corner with the older boys when he and Keith
are coming home. Keith clocks the route without asking why.

— Kizzy curls at the foot of the brothers' shared room. The building is quiet.
Outside, the block is not.

— Early morning. Ella Mae leaves for the early shift. She stops at the boys'
doorway. Watches them sleep. Pulls the door closed.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
The Woods was not just a neighborhood. It was a classroom, a warning, a playground,
and a test. It taught boys how to run, how to hide, how to laugh when nothing was
funny, and how to read a face before a word came out of a mouth.

---

### EXT. CORNER NEAR JUNKYARD — DAY

Manny and Keith stand at the edge of the junkyard, looking in.

To anyone walking past: two boys at a fence. But to Manny and Keith, this is
somewhere they have named in their heads, somewhere that belongs to them.

**KEITH**
This is our kingdom.

He says it with complete conviction. The specific seriousness of a ten-year-old
who means it.

**MANNY**
Yeah.

**KEITH**
Future emperors of the junkyard.

**MANNY**
You read that in a book or you made that up?

**KEITH**
Both.

Manny laughs. Kizzy barks at a pigeon that wanders too close.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
That was Keith. He could build an empire in his head so fast it felt real by the
time he finished the sentence.

---

### INT. ELLA MAE'S APARTMENT — EVENING

Ella Mae at the table with bills and a cup of coffee going cold. Manny sits nearby,
doing homework. Keith is already asleep.

The apartment is small enough that you can hear the building breathe — footsteps
above, a TV through the wall, the elevator catching somewhere in the shaft.

Ella Mae looks up from the bills. Looks at Manny.

**ELLA MAE**
You keeping your brother close?

**MANNY**
Yes, ma'am.

**ELLA MAE**
Not too close. He needs room.

**MANNY**
I know.

**ELLA MAE**
But close enough.

**MANNY**
I know.

She nods. Looks back at the bills. Then back at him.

**ELLA MAE**
You hear about the Reyes boy?

Manny does not respond immediately. He has heard.

**MANNY**
I heard.

**ELLA MAE**
Then you know why the rule exists.

She picks up the coffee. It's cold. She does not put it back down.

**ELLA MAE**
This house is the one thing the street doesn't get. You understand me?

**MANNY**
Yes, ma'am.

**ELLA MAE**
Good. Go to sleep.

Manny closes his book. But he does not go to sleep yet. He sits with it.

---

## ACT FOUR

### EXT. THE WOODS — GRAVEYARD NIGHT — WEEKS LATER

Night. The graveyard at the edge of the neighborhood. Trees and iron fence and the
specific dark of a place that is never fully lit.

Manny and Keith stand at the gate. Keith insisted. Manny came because Keith came.

This is the dare. The thing you do to prove you are not afraid of what is already
behind you.

**KEITH**
You go first.

**MANNY**
No.

**KEITH**
You're older.

**MANNY**
That means I know better.

**KEITH**
*(already moving)*
I'll go first.

He pushes open the iron gate. Manny catches it before it closes behind him.

They walk in together.

The graveyard is just a graveyard. Headstones, grass worn thin, a chain-link section
where newer stones wait. Quiet.

Keith stands in the middle of it and holds his arms out.

**KEITH**
I'm not scared.

**MANNY**
Okay.

**KEITH**
I'm really not scared.

**MANNY**
I believe you.

**KEITH**
Are *you* scared?

Manny looks around. The dark. The stones. The gate still slightly open behind them.

**MANNY**
Yeah.

**KEITH**
*(surprised)*
Yeah?

**MANNY**
A little.

Keith thinks about this. Then decides it is the most honest and correct answer
he has ever received.

**KEITH**
Cool.

They stand in the graveyard for another moment. Just standing in it. Proving to
themselves and each other that they can.

Kizzy waits at the gate. She will not come in.

---

### EXT. THE WOODS — CONTINUOUS — WALKING HOME

The brothers walk home through the empty streets. Late enough that the block is
quiet. Early enough that no one is asking questions.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
The graveyard bet was their thing. It always would be. Years later, when the world
had gotten complicated in ways they could not have imagined from that night, they
would still know: they had stood in the dark together. They had not run.

Keith walks close. Close enough that their shoulders occasionally touch.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Before the Rocks, before Lucky's studio, before Sarah, before the party that would
carry a name nobody wanted to say out loud — there was the Woods.

Kizzy moves at their heels.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
And the Woods, in its own way, loved them back.

---

## TAG

### EXT. THE WOODS — NIGHT

The brothers ride bikes side by side beneath the streetlights one last time.

Their building waits. Kizzy runs between their wheels.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
This is what childhood sounds like from the inside of it. Before you know it is
childhood. Before you understand what it costs to leave it behind.

The building's front door opens. The lobby light spills out.

The boys disappear into it.

Kizzy follows.

The door closes.

**MANNY (V.O.)**
Some tests you pass. Some tests change what passing means.

*(long beat)*

The Woods was still out there. It always would be.

**FADE OUT.**

**END OF EPISODE**
